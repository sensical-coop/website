Engineering software lives for a long time, and its users depend on it every day to get their results out. We manage products like these: we {{< accent >}}turn what engineers ask for into a roadmap, specifications and releases{{< /accent >}} that a development team can deliver, without breaking what existing users rely on.

{{< alert type="bg-sensical" >}}

### The problem

A mature engineering application receives many requests: a new data format, a calculation required by a new regulation, an integration with a test bench, a customer-specific report. Each one makes sense on its own. Product management is the work of choosing between them, specifying them clearly and turning them into releases that users can rely on.

{{</alert>}}

## Our experience

For several years we have managed the evolution of [UniPlot](/projects/uniplot/), a data analysis, visualisation and reporting application used by test and simulation engineers. The work covers the whole product: roadmap and requirements, coordination with developers, releases, add-ins for specific domains such as [emission computation](/projects/emission-computation/), licensing, documentation, training and the product's public material.

## Who it is for

+ **Engineering software vendors** with a mature product and a long list of customer requests.
+ **Engineering departments** that own in-house tools and need them to evolve like a product, not a collection of scripts.
+ **Technology partners** that want to connect their software to another product and need someone to own the joint scope.

## Our offer

+ **Roadmap and prioritisation**: collecting requests from users, support and sales, and deciding what goes into each release and why
+ **Requirements and specifications**: features described with use cases, acceptance criteria and test data, written so that developers can estimate and build them
+ **From one-off requests to reusable features**: deciding whether a customer request belongs in the core product, an add-in, a script or a service, and turning paid developments into something other users can benefit from
+ **Effort estimates and business cases**: work packages, build-or-integrate decisions, and the technical arguments behind a new capability
+ **Development coordination**: backlog, issue investigation, prototypes, technical validation and release preparation, working with in-house or external developers
+ **Licensing models in the product**: translating how a product is sold (perpetual, lease, floating, add-ins) into software behaviour and deployment that work for users and administrators
+ **Product consistency**: making sure the licence terms, the documentation, the privacy implications of features such as usage statistics, and the software itself say the same thing
+ **Launch material**: release notes, documentation, training, webinars, brochures and websites that explain the product to engineers
