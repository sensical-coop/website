Sensors and connected devices now produce data all the time: air quality stations, soil and water probes, energy meters, machines and vehicles in the field. Collecting that data is only the first step. We design {{< accent >}}what happens between a raw reading and a result people can trust{{< /accent >}}: quality checks, calibration, derived values, alarms and reports, automated, documented and maintained by your own team.

{{< alert type="bg-sensical" >}}

### The problem

Most sensor projects can show a graph. Far fewer can say how far to trust it. Affordable sensors drift, age and react to humidity and temperature. A calibration made at installation is applied for years, a correction lives in one person's spreadsheet, and gaps and flat lines are averaged into monthly figures without anyone noticing. When a regulator, a funder or the public asks how a number was obtained, nobody can answer with confidence.

{{</alert>}}

## How we work

We start from what exists, not from a blank page. A new workflow that changes results people already rely on, without explaining why, is not a success.

1. **Follow the data**: from the device that creates it, through the network and the platform, to the report or the decision it supports, and talk to the people who use it.
2. **Inventory it**: devices and data formats, sampling rates and timestamps, calibrations, calculations, spreadsheets, scripts and reports.
3. **Find the risks**: manual steps, duplicated formulas, missing or repeated readings, sensor drift, clock problems and checks that depend on one person.
4. **Decide what to keep**: existing calculations and reports often reflect years of decisions.
5. **Rebuild it**: quality checks, calibration and derived values run automatically as the data arrives, and reports and dashboards are produced from the same validated data.
6. **Validate it**: the new workflow runs on historical data, and its results are compared with the old ones.
7. **Hand it over**: documentation, training and a staged rollout, so that your team can maintain the workflow itself.

## Who it is for

+ **Research teams and public bodies** running sensor networks for air quality, the environment or agriculture, whose data still ends up in spreadsheets.
+ **Operators of monitoring networks** that report or publish figures, to a regulator, a funder or the public, and need to show how each number was obtained.
+ **Citizen science and community projects** that need trustworthy data from affordable sensors.

## Our offer

+ Workflow analysis: a map of how data becomes results today, with its risks and manual work
+ A target workflow and a staged plan to get there
+ Data quality: plausibility checks, gaps, flat lines and drift detection, with alarms
+ Calibration and corrections: co-location with reference instruments, calibration models and their maintenance over time
+ Derived values and indicators computed automatically as the data arrives
+ Reports and dashboards produced from the same validated data, in the formats your users need
+ Validation against historical results, and support while the old and new workflows run side by side
+ Training and documentation

## Domain knowledge

Air quality (particulate matter, gases, CO2), environmental and agricultural sensing, emission measurement and its regulations (see our [emission computation](/projects/emission-computation/) project), time-series data, LoRa mesh networks with [Mesh Things](/projects/mesh-things/), and data formats such as CSV, JSON, Parquet, NetCDF and Excel.

### Your team keeps it

The workflow belongs to the people who use it. We build it with them, using the tools they already have where possible, so that they can change a threshold, a calibration or a report without calling us. It is the same approach we take in our [co-creation](/services/co-creation/) work.
